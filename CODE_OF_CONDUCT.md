# Code of Conduct

This project is intended for a broad international education and open-source community. Project spaces must remain professional, constructive and safe for people with different identities, backgrounds, roles and levels of experience.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to repository issues, discussions, reviews, contribution channels, community spaces and project-related public interactions where a person is representing the project or its community.

## Expected behavior

Participants should:

- discuss ideas, product decisions and code on their technical/user merits;
- give specific, actionable review feedback;
- disagree with proposals without attacking people;
- respect contributor time and clearly separate blockers from preferences;
- welcome questions and avoid using expertise as a gatekeeping tool;
- respect privacy and confidentiality;
- use synthetic examples rather than real student/school data;
- report vulnerabilities through the private security process;
- respect scope/architecture decisions while challenging them constructively through evidence and ADRs.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable:

- harassment, threats, intimidation or targeted hostility;
- discriminatory or demeaning comments or conduct;
- unwanted sexualized attention;
- doxxing or publishing another person's private information;
- publishing real student, guardian, staff or school private data without authorization;
- deliberate disruption, spam or sustained bad-faith derailment;
- impersonation or deceptive attribution;
- encouraging abuse of deployed school systems;
- public disclosure of an unremediated vulnerability contrary to the coordinated security process;
- retaliation against someone for raising a good-faith conduct or security concern.

## Education-data sensitivity

Schools can hold data about children and vulnerable people. Never paste real student records, safeguarding/medical notes, credentials, private documents, production database contents or identifying screenshots into project channels merely to demonstrate a bug.

Redact or reproduce the issue with synthetic data.

## Reporting conduct concerns

For serious conduct, privacy or safety concerns, contact the repository owner privately through GitHub rather than escalating sensitive details publicly.

Security vulnerabilities follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) and [`docs/security-disclosure.md`](docs/security-disclosure.md).

When reporting a conduct concern, include the relevant interaction, date/location, impact and any context needed for fair review. Do not collect or distribute unnecessary private information about other participants.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take proportionate actions including requesting a behavior change, editing/removing content, closing or locking discussions, rejecting contributions, limiting participation or banning a participant from project-controlled spaces.

Enforcement should consider severity, context, repeated behavior, harm, acknowledgement and remediation. Maintainers should avoid publicizing sensitive report details beyond what is necessary to protect the community.

Good-faith technical disagreement, critical review and rejection of a proposal are not by themselves conduct violations.

## Maintainer responsibility

Maintainers are expected to apply these standards consistently, protect confidential reports where practical, and avoid conflicts of interest when handling a concern. If a maintainer is directly involved in a reported incident, another available maintainer/repository owner should review it where possible.
